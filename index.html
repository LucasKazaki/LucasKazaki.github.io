Featured case study · Computer vision · Independent research

Recreational Video Assistant Referee

A low-cost, single-camera system for detecting and tracking soccer players, classifying teams by jersey color, and projecting positions into a bird's-eye field representation for offside reasoning and sports analytics.

YOLOv8OpenCVK-meansTrackingHomographyPython
View public repository ↗
Case study details
  • Problem: Broadcast-grade sports tracking is expensive and assumes multiple calibrated cameras. The project explores what can be recovered from ordinary match footage.
  • System: Player detections feed a tracking and team-classification pipeline. Selected field corners define a perspective transform that maps image coordinates to a canonical field.
  • Output: Annotated footage, a live bird's-eye visualization, and movement heatmaps that make the spatial reasoning visible.
  • Learning: Geometry and data quality matter as much as model choice. Occlusion, camera motion, field visibility, and imperfect calibration define the practical limit.

Robotic manipulation

Completed

Vision-guided UR3e pick and place

An end-to-end ROS 2 pipeline that detects ArUco blocks, converts image coordinates into the robot frame, solves kinematics, and autonomously picks, places, and stacks objects.

Pipeline
Camera calibration and perspective mapping feed object pose estimates. Forward and inverse kinematics produce arm configurations, while motion and gripper commands complete a unified perception-to-action loop.

Embedded computer vision

Competition

UAV target detection on NVIDIA Jetson

YOLOv8-based detection of color-coded alphanumeric targets with different shapes, integrated with hardware, controls, and flight teams for the SUAS competition.

Engineering constraints
The work required debugging on an embedded GPU and balancing inference speed, detection quality, power, weight, camera input, and downstream interfaces. Cross-team coordination was part of the system, not an afterthought.
